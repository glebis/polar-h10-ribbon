Teaching as Intervention ↑

90 minutes of teaching produced RMSSD 61–69ms — 2.3× resting baseline, the strongest sustained parasympathetic state recorded.

RMSSD and HR across the teaching session (15-min windows). Peak vagal tone at 45–75 min, sustained with low HR.

Flow state signature. HR dropped to 59–66 bpm — the lowest sustained heart rate recorded in any context. RMSSD reached 69ms, exceeding even the post-exercise parasympathetic rebound (40ms). The combination of low HR + high RMSSD is the autonomic signature of deep cognitive engagement.

Speaking pattern. 92% of the words spoken (15,730 of 17,069) — this was active teaching, not passive listening. The parasympathetic activation occurred during output, not reception. This is consistent with Csikszentmihalyi's flow construct: challenge matched skill, full absorption, no self-referential processing.

GAD implication. The anxiety is context-dependent, not structural. Unstructured idle time = sympathetic dominance + rumination. Deep cognitive engagement = parasympathetic dominance + vagal activation. The nervous system is not broken — it's awaiting purpose.

Mechanism. Flow state reduces activity in the default mode network (DMN) — the same network hyperactive in GAD rumination. When the prefrontal cortex is fully engaged in a task, it can't simultaneously generate anxious self-referential thought. The vagal activation may be secondary to DMN suppression rather than a direct breathing/cardiac mechanism.

Research context. Peifer (2012) found flow correlates with increased vagal tone and decreased cortisol. Csikszentmihalyi's model predicts this: the challenge-skill balance in teaching eliminates the anxiety (too much challenge) and boredom (too little) that both suppress HRV.
